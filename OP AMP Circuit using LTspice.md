This is a highly complex circuit simulation project that I built in LTSpice to model the complete internal architecture of a standard operational amplifier down to the individual transistor level. The entire system runs off a dual power supply network with a positive 15 volt DC source at the top and a negative 15 volt DC source at the bottom to allow the signals to swing cleanly above and below ground. For the actual testing input I hooked up a sine wave voltage generator to the non inverting input terminal through a 10K ohm resistor while setting it to a 1 kilohertz frequency to observe the amplification behavior in real time.

The circuit design is divided into three major stages that work together to amplify the signal. The first section on the left is a comprehensive differential amplifier stage built from a network of NPN and PNP bipolar junction transistors which gives the circuit an incredibly high differential gain and a massive input impedance while successfully canceling out any common mode noise. This stage feeds directly into the middle section which functions as a high gain voltage amplifier stage featuring a 39K ohm biasing resistor and a 30 picofarad feedback capacitor that sets up a single pole frequency roll off for system stability. Finally the signal moves into the output amplifier stage on the right side which uses a push pull transistor configuration to provide massive current gain and a low output impedance alongside protective feedback resistors to prevent any damage from short circuits or current overloads.
